# OxFood — 4-hour hackathon build plan

## Context
Oxford colleges waste food at every stage: over-ordering from distributors, spoilage in storage, overproduction in the kitchen, servery leftovers and plate waste. Nothing joins these stages up. OxFood follows **every food item from distributor → kitchen → student** in one connected data model, an "ontology" in the style of Palantir Foundry. That model feeds three things:
- smarter ordering (Foodbuy CSV)
- near-effortless waste logging on a kitchen tablet (voice/camera, our own Winnow-style capture)
- recovery of surplus as paid student meals (Too Good To Go / UVA free-food-alert) or inter-college transfers

**Revision:** the organisers reminded us the product must be **feasible and able to make enough money to scale**. So this plan now includes a business model (see "Business model & feasibility", with no figures yet). An `/impact` revenue page is prepared for but only gets built when the user asks.

The repo is empty apart from a README, LICENSE and a Python .gitignore. Branch: `claude/nice-edison-a638eu`.

**Decisions made:** Python FastAPI + SQLite backend with a React frontend · Claude API on **`claude-haiku-5-5`** only (user pays personally) · demo on **one laptop, several browser windows** · all historical data fabricated by a seed script.

### Ontology (SQLModel tables)
| Object type | Key fields | Links |
|---|---|---|
| College | name, short, colour | — |
| Supplier | name, category, lead_time_days | — |
| CollegeSupplier | active | College↔Supplier (add/remove distributors) |
| Product | sku, name, category, unit, pack_size, price, storage_type (ambient/chilled/frozen), volume_l, shelf_life_days, allergens, hazard_class (raw_meat/raw_fish/ready_to_eat/allergen_nuts/…) | Supplier |
| Order / OrderLine | delivery_date, status, exported_at / qty, unit_price | College / Product |
| InventoryLot | qty, received_at, expiry_date, status (in_stock/used/transferred/wasted), source | Product, StorageUnit, Order or Transfer |
| StorageUnit | type (fridge/freezer/dry), capacity_l | College |
| Appliance | type (combi oven, blast chiller, bratt pan, fryer…) | College |
| Recipe / RecipeIngredient | meal_type, portion_g, sell_price, stages[] (prep → cook → hot-hold → servery), required appliances / qty_per_portion | Product |
| Service | date, meal (breakfast/lunch/dinner), event_type (informal/formal hall/guest night/conference), booked, attended, staff_on_shift, planned_portions | College, Recipe[] |
| Student / Attendance | pseudonymised id, year / booked, scanned_at | Service (home college only, keeps row counts sane) |
| WasteEvent | stage, weight_kg, portions_est, logged_at, method (tablet/voice/smart_bin), photo, disposition | Service, Recipe or InventoryLot |
| SurplusListing / Claim | kind (meal_to_students / stock_to_colleges), qty, price, available_until, status | College, Recipe or InventoryLot / Student or College |
| SupplierIssue | type (late/short/damaged/wrong_item/substitution/temp_breach/quality), notes, resolved | Supplier, Order, Product, substitute Product |
| Notification | audience, title, body | SurplusListing |

### Seed data (`app/seed.py`)
- Five colleges, with St Edmund Hall as the "home" college. That ties into the Winnow case study.
- **Fictional suppliers** (e.g. "Cherwell Fresh Produce", "Isis Dairy", "Thames Valley Meats", "Port Meadow Bakery", "Radcliffe Dry Goods"). That way we never pin made-up issue rates on real companies.
- About 150 products and about 25 recipes with stages and ingredients.
- An 8-week menu plan with bookings vs attendance. Show-up patterns vary: Sunday brunch low, formal hall high, exam-week dip.
- Waste events that fall after a "go-live" date, so the trend line tells a story.
- Supplier issues with substitutions, plus near-expiry lots at other colleges for the marketplace.
- A visible "Simulated data" badge in the UI.

## Pages (frontend routes)
1. **`/` Manager dashboard**
   - KPIs: kg wasted this week vs last, £ value, g per cover, % redistributed, £ recovered.
   - Charts: waste by stage / top ingredients / service & time of day / 8-week trend.
   - Alerts: lots expiring in 48h, open supplier issues, tonight's forecast vs prep plan.
   - Recharts **Sankey of food flow**: purchased → stored → cooked → served / redistributed / wasted by stage.
2. **`/tablet` Kitchen tablet** — big touch targets, minimal taps:
   - End of service: today's dishes as tiles → stage chips (built from that recipe's stages plus overproduced / servery leftover / plate waste / prep trim / spoiled) → weight keypad → Save.
   - **Voice**: hold the mic and speak (e.g. "about three kilos of curry never left the kitchen"). Browser Web Speech API (en-GB/fr-FR/es-ES) → `/api/ai/parse-waste` (Haiku) → confirm card → Save. Rule-based keyword fallback.
   - After a save, the **safety rules engine** shows allowed dispositions. A one-tap **"Offer N portions to students at £X"** creates a listing and a notification.
   - EN/FR/ES toggle, because many kitchen staff aren't native English speakers.
3. **`/bin` Smart bin (our Winnow)**
   - Webcam snapshot (getUserMedia, downscaled to 768px JPEG) plus a simulated scale reading.
   - `/api/ai/classify-photo` (Haiku vision, candidate list = today's menu) → item + stage guess → confirm. Manual tile pick as fallback.
   - Justification: [Teddy Hall's Winnow page](https://www.seh.ox.ac.uk/discover/sustainability/winnow-waste-system).
4. **`/student` Student phone view**
   - TGTG-style cards: "12 portions of chicken curry, first come first served, £2.50, Hall until 14:30".
   - Reserve → pickup code.
   - Browser Notification pop-up, plus a "free food alert" when the price is £0.
5. **`/catalogue` Catalogue & ordering**
   - Products from the college's *active* suppliers, with search/filter and a cart.
   - **"Suggested order"**: menu plan × forecast covers − in-date stock, then a storage-fit check.
   - Checkout → order record + **Foodbuy CSV download**. Columns: OrderRef, College, CostCentre, DeliveryDate, SupplierCode, SupplierName, ProductCode, Description, PackSize, Unit, Qty, UnitPrice, LineTotal. The exact format still needs confirming with Foodbuy.
6. **`/suppliers`**
   - Add/remove distributors (toggles CollegeSupplier).
   - Issue log with a form, per-supplier reliability score, most frequent issue types, substitution history.
   - **Recovery suggestions** for each issue.
7. **`/inventory`**
   - Lots with expiry countdown and storage-unit capacity bars (fridge/freezer/dry).
   - Compatibility warnings: raw meat/fish below ready-to-eat, nut-allergen segregation, frozen stays frozen.
   - "List on marketplace" for in-date, unopened stock.
8. **`/marketplace` Inter-college**
   - Other colleges' surplus stock.
   - "Fits in our freezer?" check against free capacity and compatibility → buy/transfer. This creates an InventoryLot with source=transfer.
9. **`/forecast` Bookings & covers**
   - Booked vs attended by weekday/meal/event type, show-up rate, attendance frequency distribution (pseudonymised), staff on shift.
   - Recommended portions = booked × historical show-up rate × event-type buffer. Formal hall is plated, fixed-time and high-pressure, so it gets a smaller buffer. Informal services get a larger one.
10. **`/menu` Menu & recipes**
    - Recipe stages and required appliances.
    - **Next-day breakfast/lunch suggestions.** A rule-based score (uses near-expiry lots and today's chillable surplus, cost per portion, appliances available, staff count) ranks recipes. Then one Haiku call writes the rationale and a suggested price.
11. **`/impact` ROI & revenue: DEFERRED, build only when the user says so.** Prepare for it now: `ai.py` counts AI calls and their cost, and every sale or transfer records its value, so the page can be added later without changing the data model. When it's built, it has two tabs: a college view (what the college saved and earned, minus the subscription) and an OxFood view (fee revenue across colleges, costs, margin). All prices stay as config values, with no numbers hard-coded.
12. **`/journey/:lotId` Ontology view** — the timeline for one lot: ordered → delivered (with any issue) → stored → used in recipe/service → leftover → redistributed or wasted. This is the "Foundry" moment in the pitch.

### Food-reuse rules engine (`safety_rules.py`)
Inputs: stage, left_kitchen, minutes since cooked, hot-held ≥63°C, already reheated, high-risk (rice/seafood), blast chiller available, past use-by / best-before. Outputs: allowed dispositions with reasons:
- **Plate waste / served to a diner** → no reuse; compost/AD.
- **Hot-held on servery ≥63°C within service window** → offer to students now. Otherwise discard.
- **Cooked, never left kitchen** → chill within 90 min (only if there's a blast chiller), ≤5°C, label, use within 48h (24h for rice), reheat once to ≥75°C. **Or** offer to students now.
- **Raw prep trim** → stock/soup/staff meal.
- **Unopened, in date, near expiry** → marketplace, freeze before use-by if freezable, or donate.
- **Past use-by** → discard. **Past best-before only** → usable/donatable with judgement.

Based on FSA Safer Food Better Business guidance. The UI shows "verify against your college HACCP plan". The human scientist checks the wording.

### AI integration (`ai.py`) — `claude-haiku-5-5` ($0.10 / $0.50 per M tokens)
- Official `anthropic` SDK; `client.messages.parse(..., output_format=<Pydantic model>)` for structured JSON.
- Image input = base64 `{"type":"image","source":{"type":"base64","media_type":"image/jpeg","data":…}}`.
- `output_config={"effort": "low"}`, `max_tokens` 1024 (parse/photo) / 2048 (menu). Check `stop_reason` (refusal / max_tokens) → rule fallback. Haiku 5.5 has no server-side refusal fallback.
- **Cost guard:**
  - SQLite `ai_cache` keyed by input hash, so the same input is never paid twice.
  - `AI_MAX_CALLS` hard cap and `AI_MODE=off` kill switch.
  - Every endpoint works without AI.
  - Expected total for the event: well under $0.50.
  - Also set a spend limit in the Anthropic Console.
- The key goes only in `backend/.env` on the laptop, never committed. I can't make live calls from this container, so I'll test `AI_MODE=off`. The CS teammate runs `python -m app.ai_smoke` once (about 0.1¢) to check the live path.

## Demo script (≈3 min, one laptop, three windows)
1. Problem framing (human scientist).
2. **Morning (dashboard + forecast):** "210 booked tonight, Wednesdays show 82% → cook 178". Suggested order → Foodbuy CSV. A supplier short-delivered spinach → substitution suggested.
3. **End of lunch (tablet):** a chef *speaks* the waste entry → the safety engine says "never left kitchen, offer now or blast-chill" → one tap "Offer 12 portions at £2.50".
4. **Student window:** notification pops → reserve.

6. **Dashboard updates:** kg diverted, £ recovered. Open the chicken lot's **journey**.
7. **Business model slide:** revenue streams, low costs, scale path from Oxford to contract caterers, risks answered. Add the `/impact` page to the demo here once it's built.

## Business model & feasibility (revenue that scales)
The test is whether OxFood can make enough money to grow. Our answer: *it costs a kitchen less than the waste it removes, and as software with no hardware, each extra kitchen costs us very little.* All prices and figures are left open for now. The team fills them in with real data later.

### Why a kitchen pays
Three kinds of value, each pointed out in the app:
- **Purchasing saved:** better forecasts and suggested orders mean less food bought.
- **New income:** surplus meals sold to students, and near-expiry stock sold to other colleges.
- **Waste costs avoided:** less food binned and less disposal.

### How OxFood earns money
| Stream | Model | Why it scales |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription per kitchen (core) | Monthly fee, a free pilot period, discounts for multi-site groups | Recurring and predictable. No hardware. |
| Fee on redistributed student meals | A small percentage per sale, priced to undercut Too Good To Go | Grows with use. The college keeps most of the money it would otherwise never have seen. |
| Fee on inter-college or inter-site stock transfers | A small percentage of each transfer's value | Network effect: every new kitchen adds buyers and sellers. |
| Later: food-waste reporting add-on | Monthly add-on | Regulation in Spain, France and the UK turns reporting into a must-have. |

### Why our costs stay low
Each kitchen uses its own tablet and any scale, so there's no hardware or installation. AI runs on Claude Haiku, the cheapest model, and costs very little. The main real costs are onboarding a kitchen (loading its menus, recipes and suppliers) and support.

### Scale path
1. **Oxford colleges** to prove it works: one pilot college, then a case study, then other colleges through the domestic bursars' network.
2. **Cambridge and other universities** with halls or catering: same product, same kind of buyer.
3. **Contract caterers** (e.g. Compass, Sodexo, BaxterStorey) running schools, hospitals and corporate staff restaurants: one deal covers many sites, and our Foodbuy CSV already fits Compass's ordering. This is where the real revenue is. Oxford alone is too small to be the business.
4. **France and Spain:** new laws push kitchens to cut waste, and the tablet is already in French and Spanish.

### Professional services fit
In corporate staff restaurants, hybrid working makes it hard to know how many people will eat. Desk-booking and badge-swipe data do the job of college bookings and ID scans. Multi-site estates can share stock through the same marketplace, and the waste data feeds their sustainability reporting.

### Feasibility risks and our answers
| Risk | Answer |
|---|---|
| Chefs won't log | Three taps or one spoken sentence, in their language. Teddy Hall already logs with Winnow. |
| Food-safety liability for resold meals | The college sells under its own food-safety plan. OxFood applies FSA-based rules and keeps an audit trail. |
| GDPR on ID-scan data | Pseudonymised and aggregated. Students opt in to alerts. |
| Integrations take time | No integrations needed to start: CSV out to Foodbuy, CSV in from booking systems. |
| Oxford market is small | It's the proof point. Contract caterers are the scale. |
| Competitors (Winnow, Leanpath, Too Good To Go) | Each covers one stage. We connect ordering, kitchen, redistribution and the marketplace, with no hardware. |
